The problem

SPHEREx maps the whole sky every six months.
Hard for anyone to watch it change.

  • 102 infrared colours, 0.75-5 µm, the whole sky twice a year: the right data to see nearby stars, brown dwarfs and asteroids move.
  • But each image is a 65 MB scientific FITS file, in slightly different colours from pass to pass, with no film to watch.
  • In 1930 Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto by blinking two photos. Today a student cannot blink SPHEREx.
NASA asked for

"A public-facing web tool to display images of the sky from the SPHEREx mission, making it easy for anyone to quickly see how they change over time."

Tested on Barnard's Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855, RW Aurigae and R Draconis.

2 / 7
What we built

Type a place. The real sky plays as a film.

  • Any RA/Dec or a name, or a click on the whole-sky map.
  • Film, Blink, Swipe, Time machine, Difference: every SPHEREx pass in date order.
  • Movers circled pass by pass and measured in ″/yr, with an error.
  • "How sure are we?" on every result. Nothing is called a discovery.
  • Gaia and WISE overlays, spectra, light curves, contamination flags, a link for every view.
Shown: Barnard's Star, pass 1 of 3, in the live app.

How NASA and partner data make it work

4 MB instead of 65 MB per image, lined up on Gaia.

1 · FINDIRSA TAPevery SPHEREx image of the spot, all releases (QR2, QR3, DR1, DR2)
2 · READAWS S3only the rows the field needs, from NASA's open copy
3 · CLEANFLAGSsaturation, ghosts, cosmic rays masked; zodiacal light removed
4 · ALIGNESA Gaia DR3stars moved to each image's date
5 · MEASUREOwn PSFa straight line through the passes, with errors
6 · CHECKWISE · MPCCatWISE2020, SkyBoT, JPL Horizons

All 8 challenge resources used: IRSA SPHEREx, the SPHEREx tutorial notebooks, WiseView, Gaia DR3, CSA NEOSSat, CADC, the NEOSSat tutorial and open dataset.

New releases need no code change: SPHEREx DR1 and DR2 appeared in the search by themselves. Every view exports a Jupyter notebook (Astropy) to redo the work.

What it cannot do, and says so

Planet Nine as predicted is too faint for SPHEREx (V ≈ 19-24, Batygin et al. 2019; our limit is AB 17). The test shows how deep the method reaches, not that Planet X is there.

Within reach: nearby cold brown dwarfs like WISE 0855, fast stars, asteroids. Pixels are 6.2″, so motions under 1″/yr are too small.
