See the sky move.

Pick a star. ARCNAVE shows NASA's real SPHEREx photos of that spot one after another, like a short film. If something moves, you will see it.

How it works

  1. You pick a spotA star's name, or two numbers: RA (0 to 360) and Dec (−90 to +90).
  2. ARCNAVE gets NASA's photosEvery SPHEREx photo of that spot. SPHEREx photographs the whole sky every six months, in 102 infrared colours.
  3. You watch it changeThe photos play in date order. Stars stay still; anything that moves or changes stands out and is explained in plain words.

Can I trust it?

  • Real data only. Every photo comes from NASA's archive, and every number is measured by ARCNAVE on it.
  • Checked against star catalogues. Each moving star is compared with ESA Gaia and NASA WISE. Barnard's Star: 9.9″ a year measured, 10.4″ in Gaia.
  • Tested before we looked. 18 sky fields picked in advance, with thousands of fake planets hidden and searched for. Misses are published too.
  • Nothing is called a discovery. Planet X is a hypothesis, and a Planet Nine as predicted is too faint for SPHEREx. Every result says how sure it is.
